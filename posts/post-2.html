30 Birthday Party Ideas for Kids That Are Easy, Fun and Budget-Friendly

26 Nov 2026 · Kashif Hameed

Simple themes, easy games and party food kids love — plus a planning timeline so the day feels joyful, not rushed.

Who this is for, and what it costs

This guide is for parents, grandparents, carers and guardians planning a birthday party for a child aged about 3 to 10, at home, in a garden, at a park or in a community hall in the United States, the United Kingdom or Canada. It suits 8 to 20 children, with a few adults to help, and it works for a first party as well as for a child who has had many.

Cost level: low to medium. A simple home party for 10 children typically costs around $75 to $150 in total (roughly £60 to £120, or $100 to $200 Canadian), including food, cake, decorations and a small favour for each child. A very simple party can be done for around $50, and a bigger party with a hired entertainer will cost more — we say where the extra money goes. Time: about 2 to 3 hours to plan and shop, 1 to 2 hours to set up, and 2 hours for the party itself. Most of the ideas below take 10 minutes or less to prepare.

30 ideas, grouped so you can pick and mix

Easy party themes

  1. Rainbow party. Use balloons, plates and napkins in rainbow colours, and serve colourful fruit skewers.
  2. Dinosaur party. Green and brown decorations, toy dinosaurs on the table, and a “dig for fossils” game with sand and small plastic dinosaurs.
  3. Princess and knight party. Paper crowns, a cardboard castle made from boxes, and a simple treasure hunt.
  4. Superhero party. Ask children to come as their favourite hero, or make simple capes from old fabric.
  5. Under the sea. Blue and green balloons, paper fish, and a fishing game with a magnet and paper fish.
  6. Space party. Silver and dark blue decorations, star-shaped sandwiches, and a “moon rock” hunt with foil-wrapped chocolate.

Games that actually work

  1. Musical statues. Play music, stop it, and everyone freezes. No supplies needed, and it works for ages 3 to 10.
  2. Pass the parcel. Wrap a small prize in layers of paper, with a sweet or a sticker between layers.
  3. Treasure hunt. Hide 10 clues around the house or garden, leading to a small prize.
  4. Pin the tail on the donkey — or the horn on the unicorn. A poster, a blindfold and paper tails are all you need.
  5. Balloon pop relay. Children race to sit on a balloon and pop it, then run back.
  6. Colouring and sticker station. Set up a quiet table with colouring pages, stickers and crayons for children who need a break from running around.
  7. Musical chairs, with a twist. Play music, remove a chair each round, and let the child who is out become the “DJ” who stops the music.
  8. Beanbag toss. Cut holes in a cardboard box, label them with points, and let children throw beanbags.

Party food kids love

  1. Mini sandwiches. Cut sandwiches into triangles or stars, with cheese, ham or jam.
  2. Pizza slices or mini pizzas. Buy ready-made bases and let children add their own toppings, or serve plain slices.
  3. Fruit skewers. Strawberries, grapes, melon and banana on a stick. They are colourful, healthy and easy to hold. Around $10–$12.
  4. Popcorn in paper cups. Popcorn is cheap, filling and popular. Serve it in small paper cups so it does not spill everywhere.
  5. Cupcakes instead of a big cake. Bake or buy cupcakes and let children decorate their own with icing and sprinkles.
  6. Juice boxes and water. Juice boxes are easy and safe, and water is essential.

Decorations and the cake table

  1. A balloon arch — the easy way. Tie balloons to a string or a long piece of ribbon, and hang it over a doorway.
  2. Paper bunting. Cut triangles from coloured paper and string them together.
  3. A themed tablecloth. A plain coloured tablecloth in the theme colour, with a few toy figures on top, is enough.
  4. Party hats, made at home. Cut cones from card, add elastic and stickers, and let children decorate their own.
  5. A photo corner. Hang a sheet or a curtain, add a few props — hats, glasses, signs — and let children take photos.
  6. A simple birthday cake. A one-layer sponge with icing, sprinkles and candles is enough.

Favours and thank-yous

  1. A small favour bag. A paper bag with a few stickers, a small toy, a biscuit and a balloon.
  2. A thank-you card. A short, handwritten card from the birthday child, sent the next day, is a lovely touch.
  3. A photo print. Print one photo of each child at the party and put it in their favour bag.
  4. A book instead of a bag of sweets. A small, second-hand book or a cheap paperback is a thoughtful favour that lasts longer than a bag of plastic toys.

Supply and shopping list

For a party for 10 children, with typical US prices. Buy only what you need for your chosen theme and games.

  • Balloons, pack of 20: around $5–$8
  • Paper plates, cups and napkins: around $8–$12
  • Tablecloth, bunting and paper decorations: around $10–$15
  • Cake or cupcakes, ingredients or ready-made: around $15–$25
  • Food — sandwiches, pizza, fruit, popcorn, juice: around $25–$40
  • Games — paper, stickers, crayons, small prizes: around $10–$15
  • Favour bags, around $2–$3 per child: around $20–$30 for 10
  • Candles, matches and a lighter: around $3–$5

How to plan the party, step by step

  1. Two weeks before: choose a theme and a time. Ask the birthday child to pick one theme from the list above.
  2. Ten days before: send invitations. Send a simple invitation with the date, time, address, theme and a note about allergies.
  3. One week before: shop for non-food supplies. Buy balloons, plates, decorations and favour-bag items. Make bunting and hats, and prepare the treasure-hunt clues.
  4. Two days before: shop for food and bake the cake. Buy fresh food, bake the cake or cupcakes, and store them covered.
  5. On the day, 2 hours before: set up. Put up decorations, set the food table, lay out games, and put a bin and a first-aid kit where you can reach them.
  6. During the party: follow a simple order. Free play for 15 minutes, one or two games for 30 minutes, food and cake for 30 minutes, then a quiet game and favours for the last 15 minutes.
  7. After the party: tidy with help. Ask older children to help collect plates and balloons.

Tips that save time and money

  • Keep the guest list small. Ten children is easier, cheaper and happier than twenty. A small party is not a lesser party.
  • Use one colour for decorations, plus white. It looks coordinated without buying a full themed set.
  • Make the cake the day before, and decorate it on the morning of the party. It will be fresh, and you will not be rushed.
  • Have a quiet corner with colouring and books. Some children need a break, and a quiet corner prevents tears.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Planning too many games. Three or four games in two hours is plenty. Children need free play, and too many structured games make the party feel rushed.
  • Forgetting allergies. Always ask about allergies, nuts, dairy, gluten and other needs before you buy food. Label food clearly, and keep a safe option for every child.
  • Buying everything themed. A themed plate, cup, napkin, tablecloth and banner can cost a fortune. Choose one or two themed items, and keep the rest plain and matching.
  • Starting the party hungry. Serve food early. Hungry children are tired, tearful children. Put out fruit and popcorn as soon as children arrive.

Variations for different ages, budgets and occasions

Ages 3–5: keep the party to 90 minutes, with one simple game, a colouring table and a treasure hunt with pictures, not words. Keep the music gentle and the room calm.

Ages 6–8: add a treasure hunt with written clues, a craft station and a relay race. Children this age love a challenge and a small prize.

Frequently asked questions

How long should a kids birthday party last? Two hours is ideal for ages 4 to 10. Ninety minutes is enough for ages 3 to 4. Longer parties tire children out, and parents appreciate a clear end time.

How many children should I invite? A good guide is the child’s age plus one or two — so 6 or 7 children for a 5-year-old. Invite the whole class only if you can manage it comfortably, and check your space first.
