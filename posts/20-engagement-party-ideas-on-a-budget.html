20 Engagement Party Ideas on a Budget That Look Expensive

27 Nov 2026 · Kashif Hameed

You do not need a venue, a caterer or a florist to throw a beautiful engagement party. These 20 ideas look expensive, cost very little, and work for 15 to 60 guests — mostly at home, in the garden or in a backyard.

Who this is for, and what it costs

This guide is for the couple, the family or the friends hosting an engagement party — for a small, personality-driven celebration, not a second wedding. It works in the United States, the United Kingdom and Canada, in a garden, a backyard, a living room or a rented hall, in any season.

Cost level: low to medium. Most of these ideas cost $25 to $300 in total (roughly £20 to £240, or $35 to $400 Canadian), and a whole party for 15 to 30 guests usually comes to about $20 to $50 per person at home. Time: about 2 to 3 hours to set up on the day, plus an afternoon of shopping and making things the day before. No professional help is needed — just a few friends, a car boot and a good playlist.

The 20 ideas

Set the scene

  1. A garden floral party (around $40–$75). Grocery-store roses and greenery in jars — florist-looking flowers for a fraction of the price.
  2. A backyard bonfire and s’mores station (around $30–$60). A fire pit, marshmallows, chocolate and biscuits, and blankets.
  3. A DIY balloon garland welcome (around $25–$50). Balloons in two colours, on a doorway or fence.
  4. A string-light canopy (around $20–$40). Fairy lights across the garden or ceiling.
  5. A “How We Met” photo wall (around $10–$25). Printed photos on string with pegs, first date to proposal.

Food, drink and tables

  1. A cool-blue cocktail bar (around $40–$75). One signature cocktail, one mocktail, ice and lemons.
  2. A his-side and her-side aperitif hour (around $30–$60). Two small drinks, one chosen by each of you.
  3. Picnic-style lounge tables (around $20–$40). Low tables, cushions and throws on the grass.
  4. A grazing table (around $50–$100 for 30 guests). Cheese, bread, fruit, nuts and olives on a long table — cheaper than a catered buffet, and guests help themselves.
  5. A potluck-style dinner (around $0–$50). Ask each guest to bring one dish, and you provide the drinks and the cake — the most budget-friendly dinner there is.

Details that look expensive

  1. A DIY photo booth corner (around $15–$30). A plain backdrop, a few paper props and a phone on a tripod — the photos will be better than any hired booth.
  2. Thrifted candle-cluster centrepieces (around $15–$30). Brass candlesticks and jars from thrift shops, with LED candles inside — warm, safe and very elegant.
  3. Wildflower mason jars (around $10–$20). Wildflowers or garden flowers in jam jars, tied with twine — the centrepiece that costs almost nothing.
  4. A handmade paper-fan backdrop (around $10–$20). Folded paper fans in your colours, pinned to a wall behind the cake table.
  5. A eucalyptus greenery runner (around $15–$30). A long runner of eucalyptus or olive branches down the middle of the table, with candles tucked in.

Finishing touches

  1. A kraft-paper signage station (around $5–$15). Brown paper, a black marker and a few signs — “Drinks here”, “Take a photo”, “She said yes!”
  2. Thrifted vase centrepieces (around $10–$20). Odd vases from charity shops, each with a single stem — charming because they do not match.
  3. Grocery-store florals (around $20–$40). Buy the flowers the morning of the party, trim the stems and put them in water straight away.
  4. Printable signage (around $5–$10). Print your own signs at home — a welcome sign, a menu and a drinks list, in the same font and colour.
  5. A sparkler send-off (around $10–$20). Sparklers for every guest at the end of the night, for the photo that ends the evening.

Supply and shopping list

You do not need to buy everything below — this is a checklist for one party, for 15 to 30 guests. Typical US prices are shown; prices vary by shop and season.

  • Flowers and greenery (grocery store): around $30–$60
  • Balloons and garland tape: around $15–$30
  • String lights (battery or plug-in): around $15–$30
  • Candles and LED candles: around $10–$20
  • Tablecloths, napkins and cups: around $15–$30
  • Food and drink (grazing table or potluck): around $50–$100
  • Photo printing for the photo wall: around $10–$20
  • Sparklers: around $10–$20

How to plan it, step by step

  1. Pick a date and a guest list first. Keep it to the people you will invite to the wedding — 15 to 60 guests — and pick a date at least three weeks away.
  2. Choose one theme and two colours. Garden floral, backyard bonfire or cocktail evening — and two colours for everything, from the balloons to the napkins.
  3. Book the space. A garden or a living room is free; a hall needs booking early. Check the weather plan if you are outside.
  4. Order the food and drink. A grazing table or a potluck is the cheapest good food. Order the cake a week ahead.
  5. Buy the decorations the week before. Balloons, lights, candles and paper goods — and the flowers on the morning of the party.
  6. Set up the day before where you can. The photo wall, the backdrop and the table settings can all be done the night before, so the day itself is calm.

Tips that save time and money

  • Borrow before you buy — tables, chairs, string lights and vases from friends and family cost nothing and look better than new ones.
  • Buy the flowers at a grocery store on the morning of the party. They are fresh, cheap and just as pretty as florist flowers in a jar.
  • Use LED candles everywhere. They are safe, reusable and look just as warm in photos as real candles.
  • Ask one friend to be the photographer for the evening, with a phone on a tripod at the photo booth — you will actually get the photos.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Inviting people you will not invite to the wedding. It creates an awkward conversation later. Keep the list to wedding guests.
  • Spending on the venue instead of the food. Guests remember good food and a warm welcome, not an expensive room.
  • Too many colours. Pick two colours and stick to them — three or four colours make a party look messy, not rich.
  • Leaving the flowers until the last minute. Buy them in the morning, trim the stems and put them in water straight away, or they will wilt by evening.

Variations for different budgets, ages and occasions

Around $75: a garden party for 15 guests — grocery-store flowers, string lights, a grazing board and a homemade cake. Around $150: add a balloon garland, a cocktail bar and a photo booth. Around $300: a backyard party for 40 guests, with a bonfire, a full grazing table and sparklers.

Indoors: swap the bonfire for a candle-lit living room, the balloon garland for a paper-fan backdrop, and the picnic tables for a long dining table. In winter: add throws, hot chocolate and a fire — the same ideas, just warmer.

Frequently asked questions

How much does an engagement party cost on a budget? A budget engagement party at home for 15 to 30 guests usually costs $75 to $300 in total, or about $20 to $50 per person. The biggest savings come from the venue (your garden), the food (potluck or a grazing table) and the flowers (grocery-store bunches).

Who pays for an engagement party? Traditionally the bride’s family hosts, but today it is often the couple, both families together, or close friends. Whoever hosts decides the budget — and a small, lovely party at home is always better than a big, stressed one.

How many guests should an engagement party have? Keep it to the people you will invite to the wedding — usually 15 to 60 guests. A smaller guest list is the easiest way to keep the cost down and the party feeling personal.
