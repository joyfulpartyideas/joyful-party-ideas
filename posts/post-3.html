20 DIY Party Favours Guests Will Actually Keep

26 Nov 2026 · Kashif Hameed

Skip the plastic trinkets. These handmade favours are quick to make, cheap to put together and lovely enough that guests take them home happily.

Who this is for, and what it costs

This guide is for anyone hosting a birthday, baby shower, bridal shower, wedding, holiday party or family gathering in the United States, the United Kingdom or Canada, and wondering what to give guests as they leave. It suits favours for children, for adults, or for a mixed group, and it works for 10 to 30 guests without a big budget or a craft room.

Cost level: low. Most favours here cost around $1 to $3 each (roughly £1 to £2.50, or $1.50 to $4 Canadian), including the bag or box. A set of 20 favours typically costs around $20 to $50 in total. Time: about 30 minutes to shop, and 1 to 2 hours to make 20 favours — most take 3 to 5 minutes each once you have a simple assembly line. You do not need craft experience; if you can tie a bow and fill a bag, you can make every favour below.

The 20 ideas

Edible favours — always popular

  1. Mini jam or honey jars. Small jars of jam or honey, around $1–$2 each in a pack, with a ribbon and a tag.
  2. Homemade cookies in a bag. Two or three cookies in a clear bag, tied with twine.
  3. Hot chocolate kits. A small bag with cocoa, mini marshmallows and a candy cane, with a tag that says “Just add milk.” Around $1–$2 each.
  4. Popcorn cones. Fill paper cones with popcorn and a sprinkle of salt or cinnamon sugar.
  5. Tea bags in a pretty pouch. Two good tea bags in a small paper pouch, with a tag.
  6. Small chocolate bars, wrapped. Buy chocolate bars in a pack, wrap each one in pretty paper, and add a thank-you tag.

Useful favours — guests will use them

  1. Mini hand cream and lip balm. A small hand cream and a lip balm in a tiny bag.
  2. Seed packets for the garden. A packet of wildflower or herb seeds, with a tag that says “Thank you for helping us grow.” Around $1–$2 each.
  3. Small candles in tins. Mini candles in small tins, around $1.50–$2.50 each in a pack.
  4. Personalised bookmarks. Print or write a favourite quote on card, laminate it if you can, and add a ribbon.
  5. Mini notebooks and pencils. A small notebook and a pencil, tied together, for around $1–$2 each.
  6. Bath salts in a small jar. Fill small jars with Epsom salts and a few dried flowers, and add a label.

Keepsakes — small, but worth keeping

  1. A photo from the party. Print one photo of each guest, taken during the party, and put it in a small frame or a paper holder.
  2. A handmade keyring. A simple keyring with a bead, a charm or a tassel, made in the party colours.
  3. A small potted succulent. A tiny succulent in a small pot, around $2–$3 each in a pack.
  4. A cookie cutter with a recipe card. A star, heart or Christmas-tree cutter, with a short recipe on a card.
  5. A friendship bracelet. A simple braided bracelet in the party colours, made from embroidery thread.
  6. A small bag of confetti or dried petals. A paper bag of dried petals or biodegradable confetti, with a tag.
  7. A mini photo magnet. A small photo, printed at home, stuck to a magnet strip and put in a paper sleeve.
  8. A thank-you note, handwritten. A short, honest note, written by hand, in an envelope with a small treat.

Supply and shopping list

For 20 favours, using the favour-bag example below. Typical US prices; buy in packs where you can.

  • Paper bags, small, pack of 20: around $4–$6
  • Twine or ribbon, one roll: around $3–$5
  • Gift tags, pack of 20: around $3–$5
  • Tissue paper, one pack: around $2–$3
  • Favour contents, such as cookies, tea, seeds or small toys: around $15–$30 for 20
  • Stickers or a stamp for the tags: around $3–$5
  • Optional: a small box or tray to display the favours: around $3–$5

How to make them, step by step — a simple favour bag

  1. Choose one favour idea and one bag. Do not try to make three different favours.
  2. Make one favour first, as a test. Fill one bag, tie it, add a tag, and look at it from a guest’s point of view.
  3. Set up an assembly line. Lay out all the bags in a row, put the contents in bowls in the middle, and fill each bag in turn.
  4. Add a tag with a thank-you and a date. Write “Thank you for coming — [Name]’s party, 26 Nov 2026” or a short message that suits the occasion.
  5. Check for allergies and safety. If the favour is edible, label nuts, dairy, gluten and other allergens.
  6. Display them neatly. Put the finished favours in a basket, on a tray or on a table near the door, so guests can take one as they leave.
  7. Make two spares. Make 22 favours for 20 guests.

Tips that save time and money

  • Choose a favour you can make in bulk. One idea, made 20 times, is far easier than 20 different ideas.
  • Buy bags, tags and ribbon in packs of 20 or 50. It is cheaper, and you will have spares for the next party.
  • Make favours the night before the party, not on the day. You will be tired on the day, and a rushed favour looks rushed.
  • Use what you already have. Jam jars, ribbon, paper and string from home can save you $10 or more.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Choosing a favour that breaks easily. A fragile ornament or a thin glass jar will break in a car or a bag. Choose something sturdy, or wrap it well.
  • Forgetting allergies. Always ask about allergies before you choose an edible favour, and label it clearly. A nut cookie can be dangerous for a child with a nut allergy.
  • Making the favour too big. A large favour costs more, takes longer to make, and is harder to carry. Small and lovely beats big and awkward.
  • Leaving it to the last minute. Favours take longer than you think. Start the night before, and you will enjoy making them.

Variations for different budgets, ages, themes and occasions

For children’s birthdays: choose popcorn cones, cookies, mini notebooks, friendship bracelets or a small toy in a bag. Keep it colourful, sturdy and safe for the age group.

For a baby shower: choose seed packets, bath salts, small candles, tea bags or a photo magnet. Keep the colours soft and the message gentle.

Frequently asked questions

Do I have to give a favour at every party? No. A favour is a lovely thank-you, but it is not required. If your budget is tight, a handwritten thank-you card is enough, and guests will not miss a favour.

How much should I spend per favour? Around $1 to $3 per favour is typical for a birthday or a baby shower, and around $3 to $5 for a wedding. The right amount is the one you can afford comfortably for every guest.

What is the best favour for children? Something they can use or eat — a cookie, a notebook, a friendship bracelet or a small toy. Avoid tiny parts for children under three, and always check for allergies.
