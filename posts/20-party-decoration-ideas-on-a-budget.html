20 Party Decoration Ideas on a Budget That Look Expensive

27 Nov 2026 · Kashif Hameed

Expensive-looking parties are not about spending more — they are about spending in the right places. These 20 ideas work for birthdays, baby showers and holiday parties, with plans for $25, $75 and $150.

Who this is for, and what it costs

This guide is for anyone decorating a party at home — a birthday, a baby shower, an engagement party or a holiday party — in the United States, the United Kingdom or Canada. It is year-round, not just for Christmas, and it works in a living room, a garden or a rented hall.

Cost level: low. There are three plans in this guide: $25 for a small party, $75 for a full party, and $150 for a big celebration (roughly £20, £60 and £120, or $35, $100 and $200 Canadian). Time: about 2 to 3 hours to set up on the day, plus an afternoon of shopping the week before. No crafting skill is needed — most of these ideas are folding, tying, pouring or plugging in.

The 20 ideas

The two rules that make everything work

  1. Pick two colours and commit. Two colours, used everywhere, look designed. Five colours look like a clearance sale.
  2. Spend first on lights and one focal point. Fairy lights and one focal point do more than ten small decorations.
  3. Plan zone by zone. Door, table, drinks, light and photo corner — one idea per zone.
  4. Shop your home first. Throws, scarves, cushions, jars and fairy lights you already own.
  5. Reuse and store for next year. Buy things you can fold, wash and store — next year’s party costs half as much.

The table

  1. Layered paper tablecloths and gold chargers. A paper tablecloth, a runner, and gold chargers under plain plates.
  2. LED pillar candles and a cranberry vase centrepiece. Tall LED candles in jars, with a vase of cranberries in the middle.
  3. Thrifted brass candlesticks and silver trays. Charity-shop brass and silver, with candles on top.
  4. Grocery-store greenery and pinecones. Eucalyptus or pine branches down the table, with pinecones tucked in.
  5. Ribbon on everything. The same ribbon, tied on chairs, jars and favours.

The room

  1. A balloon garland. Balloons in your two colours, over a doorway or along a wall.
  2. A foil fringe photo corner. Foil fringe curtains, a few balloons and a sign — under $15.
  3. Paper snowflakes and a dried-orange garland. Paper snowflakes and dried orange slices on string.
  4. Battery fairy lights in jars. Fairy lights in jam jars, on windowsills and the drinks table.
  5. Printable signs. Print your own signs at home — welcome, menu and drinks list.

The finishing touches

  1. Shop-your-home textiles. Throws, scarves and cushions — textiles make a room feel warm.
  2. A coffee-table ornament bowl. Baubles or pinecones in a big bowl — thirty seconds.
  3. Plain baubles, deep in the tree or the arrangement. Plain baubles deep inside, special ones outside.
  4. A kids’ craft corner. Paper, crayons and stickers on a small table.
  5. A bathroom mini-wreath. A small wreath on the bathroom door.

The $25, $75 and $150 plans

$25 — small party: two-colour balloons ($8), fairy lights in jars ($5), paper tablecloth and ribbon ($7), and greenery from the garden ($0). Decorate the door and the table only.

$75 — full party: everything in the $25 plan, plus a balloon garland ($20), gold chargers and LED candles ($15), and a foil fringe photo corner ($15). Decorate the door, table, drinks, light and photo corner.

$150 — big celebration: everything in the $75 plan, plus thrifted brass and trays ($25), a eucalyptus runner ($20), printable signs and a kids’ craft corner ($15), and a second set of lights ($15). Every zone, fully dressed.

Supply and shopping list

You do not need to buy everything below — this is a checklist for one party. Typical US prices are shown; prices vary by shop and season.

  • Balloons and garland tape: around $10–$20
  • Fairy lights (battery): around $5–$15
  • Paper tablecloths, napkins and ribbon: around $10–$20
  • LED pillar candles: around $8–$15
  • Gold chargers (set of 8): around $10–$15
  • Greenery and pinecones: around $0–$10
  • Foil fringe curtains: around $5–$10
  • Printable signs (paper and ink): around $5–$10

How to decorate, step by step

  1. Pick your two colours. Look at what you already own — a tablecloth, a set of napkins, a favourite scarf — and build the colours around that.
  2. Walk the house with a basket. Collect throws, jars, trays, candles and lights before you buy anything. You will be surprised how much you already have.
  3. Buy the big things first. Balloons, lights and tablecloths — the things that set the look — then the small things.
  4. Set up the table first. Tablecloth, runner, chargers, candles, centrepiece — once the table looks right, the rest of the room follows.
  5. Do the door and the photo corner next. The door is the first thing guests see; the photo corner is the thing they photograph.
  6. Turn the lights on last, and step back. Lights change everything. Turn them on, step back to the door, and look at the room as a guest will see it.

Tips that save time and money

  • Buy balloons, ribbon and lights in bulk — it costs less per party.
  • Use the same two colours all year, changing one accent.
  • Borrow the big pieces — a balloon pump, a cake stand, a punch bowl — from friends instead of buying them.
  • Take a photo of the finished room before guests arrive. It is your record for next time, and it is the photo you will actually share.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Too many colours. Two colours look expensive; five colours look cheap. Pick two and commit.
  • Decorating everywhere, thinly. Five zones, one idea each, beats twenty small decorations scattered around the room.
  • Buying everything new. Shop your home first, borrow second, and buy only what is left.
  • Forgetting the light. A room with no fairy lights looks flat in photos, however much you spent on the table. Lights first.

Variations for different budgets, ages and occasions

Birthday: add a balloon garland and a photo corner, and let the birthday person pick the two colours. Baby shower: use soft colours, wildflower jars and a paper-fan backdrop. Holiday party: add greenery, pinecones and dried oranges, and warm white lights everywhere.

Frequently asked questions

How can I decorate a party cheaply and make it look expensive? Pick two colours and use them everywhere, spend first on lights and one focal point like a balloon garland or a dressed table, and fill the rest with dollar-store and thrifted finds. Restraint looks expensive; a dozen different colours looks cheap.

How much should I spend on party decorations? A small party at home can look lovely for $25, a full party for $75, and a big celebration for $150. Start with the $25 plan in this guide and add one zone at a time — door, table, drinks, light and photo corner — as your budget allows.

What is the cheapest way to decorate for a party? Shop your home first: throws, scarves, cushions, jars, candles and fairy lights you already own. Then add one new thing per zone from a dollar store, and borrow the big pieces like a balloon pump or a cake stand from friends.
