20 Easy Christmas Party Food and Drink Ideas Guests Will Love

27 Nov 2026 · Kashif Hameed

Good Christmas party food is not complicated — it is a grazing board, a slow cooker doing the work, and a hot-chocolate bar that everyone gathers around. These 20 ideas take mostly under 30 minutes, and feed 8 to 20 guests.

Who this is for, and what it costs

This guide is for anyone hosting a Christmas party at home — for family, friends, neighbours or colleagues — in the United States, the United Kingdom or Canada. The ideas work for 8 guests or for 20, for an evening party or an afternoon one, and for a crowd that includes children, vegetarians and guests with allergies.

Cost level: low to medium. Most dishes cost $10 to $30 to make (roughly £8 to £25, or $15 to $40 Canadian), and a whole party for 20 guests usually comes to about $8 to $12 per person for food and drink. Time: most ideas take under 30 minutes of active cooking, plus time in the oven or the slow cooker. Almost everything can be made ahead — only the hot-chocolate bar needs setting up on the day.

The 20 ideas

To start: grazing and bites

  1. A grazing and charcuterie board (around $25–$40 for 20 guests). Cheese, meats, crackers, grapes, nuts and olives — 20 minutes to arrange.
  2. Cranberry-brie bites (around $10–$15). Puff pastry, brie and cranberry sauce in mini muffin tins.
  3. Pigs in blankets (around $8–$12). Sausages wrapped in bacon, roasted until crisp.
  4. A cheese board with chutney (around $15–$25). Three cheeses, a chutney, crackers and grapes.
  5. A festive salad (around $10–$15). Greens, pomegranate, orange and nuts, with a light dressing.

Hot mains that cook themselves

  1. Slow-cooker meatballs (around $12–$18). Meatballs in cranberry or barbecue sauce, kept warm in the slow cooker.
  2. Baked ham sliders (around $15–$20). Ham and cheese on small rolls, baked with butter and mustard.
  3. Roast-potato bites (around $8–$12). Crispy roast potatoes with rosemary and sea salt, with a dip.
  4. A soup station (around $10–$15). A big pot of tomato or squash soup, with bread and toppings.
  5. A labelled allergy-friendly tray (around $10–$15). A separate nut-free, gluten-free tray, with its own label and spoon.

Drinks: the hot-chocolate bar and more

  1. A hot-chocolate bar (around $15–$25). Hot chocolate in a slow cooker, with marshmallows, cream and candy canes.
  2. Mulled cider and a mocktail (around $10–$15). Warm cider with orange and cinnamon, plus a cranberry mocktail.
  3. A cranberry spritz (around $8–$12). Cranberry juice, soda and rosemary, in a big jug.
  4. Festive punch (around $10–$15). Apple juice, cranberry juice and ginger ale, with orange slices.
  5. Coffee and tea, done properly (around $5–$10). A big pot of coffee, good tea and milk.

Desserts to finish

  1. Mini mince pies (around $8–$12). Shop-bought pastry, mincemeat and icing sugar.
  2. Gingerbread cookies (around $8–$12). Baked the day before, decorated with the children.
  3. Peppermint brownies (around $10–$15). Brownies with crushed candy canes on top.
  4. Trifle cups (around $10–$15). Sponge, custard, fruit and cream in small cups.
  5. Candy-cane bark and a fruit platter with dip (around $10–$15). Melted chocolate with crushed candy canes, plus a fruit platter with dip.

Supply and shopping list

You do not need to buy everything below — this is a checklist for one party, for 8 to 20 guests. Typical US prices are shown; prices vary by shop and season.

  • Grazing board (cheese, meats, crackers, fruit, nuts): around $25–$40
  • Hot dishes (meatballs, sliders, potatoes, soup): around $30–$50
  • Drinks (hot chocolate, cider, spritz, coffee): around $20–$30
  • Desserts (mince pies, cookies, brownies, trifle): around $25–$40
  • Plates, napkins, cups and cutlery: around $10–$15
  • Labels and a marker for the allergy tray: around $2–$3
  • Optional: a slow cooker, if you do not have one: around $25–$35

How to plan it, step by step

  1. Count the guests and pick a menu. For 8 guests, pick one grazing board, one hot dish, one drink and one dessert. For 20, pick two of each.
  2. Ask about allergies in the invitation. One line — “Please let me know about any allergies” — saves a lot of worry on the night.
  3. Make the desserts two days ahead. Brownies, bark and trifle cups all keep well in the fridge, and they are one less thing to do on the day.
  4. Make the hot dishes the day before. Meatballs, soup and sliders can all be reheated, and they taste better the next day.
  5. Build the grazing board on the morning of the party. It takes 20 minutes, and it should look fresh when guests arrive.
  6. Set up the hot-chocolate bar last, just before guests arrive. Slow cooker on, toppings in little bowls, mugs in a row — and you are done.

Tips that save time and money

  • Use the slow cooker for everything — it cooks and keeps things warm.
  • Buy the cheese, the bread and the fruit at a grocery store on the morning of the party. They are fresh, cheap and just as good as deli counter versions.
  • Make one big dessert table instead of five small desserts. One table, five dishes, and guests help themselves.
  • Keep a spare bottle of soda and a spare bag of ice in the fridge. They are the two things that always run out first.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Too much food, too little variety. Six small dishes beat one huge dish — guests like to try a little of everything.
  • Forgetting the allergy tray. Label everything, keep one tray separate, and use a different serving spoon for it.
  • Everything hot, nothing cold. A grazing board and a fruit platter give guests something to eat while the hot dishes are still cooking.
  • Running out of cups. Count the mugs and the glasses before the party, not during it — and buy paper cups as a backup.

Variations for different budgets, ages and occasions

For 8 guests: one grazing board, one hot dish, the hot-chocolate bar and one dessert — about $60 in total. For 20 guests: double everything, and add a second slow cooker — about $120 in total.

For children: add a fruit platter, gingerbread cookies and a non-alcoholic spritz, and keep the spicy food on a separate tray. For a vegetarian crowd: swap the meatballs for roasted vegetables, the ham sliders for cheese sliders, and the pigs in blankets for halloumi bites.

Frequently asked questions

How much food do I need for a Christmas party for 20 guests? Plan on about 6 to 8 bites per person for a party with drinks, plus one main dish if it is a meal. For 20 guests that means roughly 150 bites across the grazing board, the hot dishes and the desserts — and it is always better to have a little too much than too little.

What food can I make ahead for a Christmas party? Almost everything in this guide can be made ahead: the grazing board the morning of the party, the slow-cooker meatballs and the soup the day before, and the desserts — brownies, bark and trifle cups — up to two days ahead. Only the hot-chocolate bar needs setting up on the day.

What should I serve at a Christmas party for guests with allergies? Label everything, keep one tray completely nut-free and gluten-free, and put the allergy-friendly tray at the far end of the table so it does not get mixed up. Ask guests about allergies in the invitation, and keep the labels short and clear.
