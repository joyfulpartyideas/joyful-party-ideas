25 Cozy Christmas Party Decoration Ideas on a Budget

26 Nov 2026 · Kashif Hameed

Warm lights, simple greenery and clever reuse — make your home feel festive for a Christmas party without spending a fortune.

Who this is for, and what it costs

This guide is for anyone hosting a Christmas party at home — a family gathering, a friends’ party, a neighbourhood get-together or a small office party — in the United States, the United Kingdom or Canada. It suits a living room, a dining room or a small hall, for 10 to 30 guests, and it works whether you have a big tree or no tree at all.

Cost level: low. Most ideas here cost around $3 to $15, and a whole room can be decorated for around $50 to $100 in total (roughly £40 to £80, or $65 to $130 Canadian) if you buy a few new items and reuse the rest. Many ideas are free if you use greenery, paper, fabric and lights you already own. Time: about 2 to 3 hours to decorate a living room and a table, plus 30 minutes to tidy up afterwards. Most ideas take 10 to 20 minutes each.

The 25 ideas

Warm lights — the easiest win

  1. Fairy lights in jars. Put a string of battery fairy lights in a glass jar, a vase or a lantern.
  2. Lights along a shelf or a mantel. Drape a string of warm white lights along a shelf, a mantelpiece or a staircase.
  3. Candles in safe holders. Use candles in glass holders, lanterns or jars, and keep them away from curtains, paper and children.
  4. A lit wreath on the door. Add a small string of battery lights to a wreath on your front door.
  5. Lights in the window. Hang a string of lights in a front window, so your home glows from the street.

Simple greenery — free and festive

  1. Pine branches on the table. Lay a few pine or fir branches down the centre of the table, with a few pine cones and candles.
  2. A garland over a doorway. Drape a garland of greenery over a doorway or a mirror, and add a few baubles or a ribbon.
  3. Greenery in vases. Put a few sprigs of pine, holly or eucalyptus in a vase or a jar.
  4. A wreath you make yourself. Bend a wire coat hanger into a circle, tie on pine branches with twine, and add a ribbon.
  5. Pine cones as decoration. Put pine cones in a bowl, on a tray or on the table, with a few fairy lights.

The Christmas table

  1. A plain tablecloth in red, green or cream. A plain tablecloth in a festive colour, with a simple runner, looks better than a busy printed one.
  2. Paper stars or snowflakes. Cut stars or snowflakes from white or gold paper, and hang them from the ceiling or lay them on the table.
  3. Place cards with a sprig of greenery. Write each guest’s name on a card, and tuck a small sprig of pine or holly behind it.
  4. A centrepiece in a tray. Put candles, pine cones and baubles on a tray in the centre of the table.
  5. Gold and red napkins. Use cloth napkins in red or gold, or paper napkins in a festive colour, tied with twine and a sprig of greenery.
  6. A small tree on the table. A small potted tree, around 30–45 cm (12–18 in) tall, on the table or on a sideboard, with a few baubles and lights.

The tree and the room

  1. Baubles in one or two colours. Choose baubles in two colours — red and gold, or silver and blue — and use them on the tree, in a bowl and on the garland.
  2. Ribbon on the tree. Drape a wide ribbon around the tree, instead of tinsel.
  3. Paper chains. Make paper chains from red, green and gold paper, and hang them across a room or along a staircase.
  4. Stockings on the mantel. Hang stockings on a mantelpiece, a shelf or a staircase, even if you do not fill them.
  5. A cosy corner with blankets. Put a basket of blankets and cushions near the seating area, so guests can get cosy.

Entryway, food table and finishing touches

  1. A welcome sign on the door. A simple chalkboard or paper sign that says “Merry Christmas — come in!” Around $3–$5, or free if you write it by hand.
  2. A drinks station with a festive sign. Set up a small table with drinks, glasses and a sign, with a few baubles and a string of lights.
  3. Cinnamon sticks and orange slices. Put cinnamon sticks and dried orange slices in a bowl, on the table or in a garland.
  4. A “thank you” corner. Put a small basket by the door with a few favours — cookies, a small candle or a hot chocolate kit — for guests to take home.
  5. Reuse last year’s decorations. Before you buy anything new, look in your decoration box.

Supply and shopping list

For a living room and a table for 15 guests, with typical US prices. Buy only what you need, and reuse what you already own.

  • Fairy lights, 2 strings: around $10–$16
  • Candles and holders: around $8–$12
  • Greenery, pine branches and pine cones: around $5–$10, or free from a garden
  • Baubles, pack of 20: around $10–$15
  • Ribbon, twine and paper: around $5–$8
  • Tablecloth and napkins: around $12–$18
  • A small tree or a garland: around $10–$20
  • Place cards and a welcome sign: around $3–$5

How to decorate, step by step

  1. Choose two colours. Red and gold, green and gold, or silver and blue are classic and easy.
  2. Start with lights. Put up fairy lights first — on the tree, on a shelf, in jars and in the window.
  3. Add greenery next. Lay pine branches on the table, hang a garland over a doorway, and put greenery in vases.
  4. Decorate the tree. Put on lights, then ribbon, then baubles, then a star or an angel on top.
  5. Set the table last. Put on the tablecloth, lay out plates and glasses, add a centrepiece, place cards and napkins.
  6. Add the finishing touches. Put out candles, a welcome sign, a drinks station and a basket of blankets.
  7. Do a safety check. Check that candles are away from curtains and paper, that lights are not overloaded, that cables are not a trip hazard, and that the room is clear for guests to move around.

Tips that save time and money

  • Decorate the day before the party, not on the day. You will be calmer, and the room will look better.
  • Use warm white lights, not bright white or coloured lights, for a cosy feel. One colour of light looks better than a mix.
  • Buy decorations in the January sales, and store them carefully for next year. You can save 50% or more.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Using too many colours. Five colours, three patterns and two types of lights in one room look messy. Choose two colours and stick to them.
  • Putting candles near paper or curtains. Candles are beautiful, but they are a fire risk. Keep them in glass holders, away from paper, curtains and children, and never leave them burning unattended.
  • Overloading the table. A table full of decorations leaves no room for food, plates and glasses. Keep the centrepiece low and small, and leave space for guests.
  • Buying everything new. Before you buy, look in your decoration box, your garden and your kitchen. Many of the best decorations are things you already own.

Variations for different budgets, spaces and occasions

On a tight budget (around $20): use fairy lights you already own, pine branches from a garden, paper snowflakes and candles in jars. The room will still feel festive, and you will have spent almost nothing.

For a bigger budget (around $150): add a garland, a small tree, a new tablecloth and a set of baubles in your two colours. Keep the same simple plan — lights, greenery, tree, table — and the room will look polished, not overdone.

Frequently asked questions

How much should I spend on Christmas decorations? Around $50 to $100 is typical for a living room and a table if you are buying a few new items. If you reuse lights and baubles from last year, around $20 to $30 is enough for greenery, candles and paper.

When should I decorate for a Christmas party? Decorate the day before the party, so you are not rushed on the day. Put up the tree a week or two before Christmas, and take decorations down in early January.
